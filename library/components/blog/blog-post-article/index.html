Five things to do before the first hard freeze

By Marcus Webb, owner of AAA Home Specialists · October 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Every November we get the same calls: a burst spigot, a frozen line in the crawl space, a heat pump that quit on the coldest night. Most of them are preventable with an hour on a Saturday. Here is the hour.

1. Disconnect and drain every hose

A hose left on a spigot traps water inside the valve. When that water freezes, it splits the pipe behind the wall, and you will not know until spring when you turn it on and the inside wall gets wet. Unscrew the hose, open the spigot for a second to drain it, and put a foam cover on.

2. Close the crawl space vents

Open vents are great in July and terrible in January. Cold air moving under the house is what freezes supply lines. Most vents have a sliding damper; if yours do not, a piece of rigid foam cut to size does the job.

3. Set the thermostat and leave it

If you travel, do not turn the heat off. Set it to 55 and leave the cabinet doors under the kitchen and bathroom sinks open so warm air reaches the pipes on exterior walls.

The most expensive freeze damage we see every year is in houses where somebody was trying to save forty dollars on the gas bill.

Marcus Webb

4. Check the heat pump before you need it

Run it in heat mode for ten minutes on a mild day. If it blows cool air or short-cycles, that is a service call now, not on the first 18-degree night when every HVAC tech in Pope County is booked solid.

5. Know where your main shutoff is

  • In most Russellville homes it is at the meter near the street, or where the line enters the house.
  • Put a tag on it. Show everyone in the house how to turn it.
  • If it is stuck, call us before winter; a seized valve during a leak is a bad combination.

That is the whole list. If you would rather we do it, our winterization visit covers all five plus a water heater check, and takes about an hour.
