Color help

How to pick a paint color you will still like in five years.

Most color regret comes from choosing off a two-inch chip under store lighting. Here is how Fresh Coat Painting & Drywall helps customers choose, and what we have learned from five thousand rooms.

Start with what is staying

The floor, the counters, the sofa and the brick on the fireplace are not changing, so the paint has to get along with them. Pull the undertone out of the biggest fixed thing in the room: most Arkansas oak floors lean orange, most gray tile leans blue. A wall color with the opposite undertone fights it; one with the same undertone disappears into it. You usually want something in between.

Then look at the light. North-facing rooms run cool and gray all day, so a white that looks crisp in the store goes icy. South-facing rooms run warm and bright and can take a cooler color. Ask us for the free color consult and we bring sample pots, not chips.

Test it the right way

  • Paint two coats on a two-foot square of poster board, not the wall, so you can move it around the room.
  • Look at it morning, noon and night, with the lamps on and off. Colors shift more than you expect.
  • Hold it next to the trim, the floor and the biggest piece of furniture. That is where the clash shows up.
  • Narrow to two, never five. Five samples on one wall only make all of them look wrong.

Nobody has ever regretted a color one shade quieter than they thought they wanted. Plenty of people have regretted one shade louder.

Sheen matters as much as color

  1. Flat or matte on ceilings and low-traffic walls: hides every roller mark and drywall flaw, but scuffs show.
  2. Eggshell or satin on most walls, kitchens and kids' rooms: washable, with a soft glow that does not spotlight dents.
  3. Semi-gloss on trim, doors and cabinets: wipes clean, and the contrast with matte walls is what makes trim look crisp.

The same color in two sheens reads as two different colors. If you are matching an existing wall, tell us the sheen too.

Palettes that work here

What River Valley homes are asking for this year.

Warm whites and soft greiges, with one darker room.

The all-gray decade is over. Most whole-house jobs this year are a warm white or a light greige through the main rooms, the same white on trim in semi-gloss, and one room, usually a study or dining room, in something deep.

Book a color consult
  • Main rooms: a warm white or pale greige, eggshell
  • Trim and doors: the same white, semi-gloss
  • Ceilings: flat, one shade lighter than the walls
  • One accent room: deep green, navy or charcoal
  • Bedrooms: a muted blue-green or soft clay

Two-tone cabinets and a wall that stays quiet.

Painted cabinets are our most-requested kitchen job. Dark lowers with white uppers is still the favorite, but the walls should step back: a pale, warm neutral that lets the cabinets and the backsplash do the talking.

Quote a cabinet job
  • Lowers: deep green, navy or black, satin
  • Uppers: a soft white that matches the trim
  • Island: a third color only if the room is big
  • Walls: pale warm neutral, eggshell
  • Hardware: swap it; it is the cheapest upgrade in the room

Three colors, chosen for brick and Arkansas sun.

Most exteriors from Fresh Coat Painting & Drywall use three colors: body, trim and door. Dark bodies look great but fade faster in full sun; we steer south-facing houses toward mid-tones. Brick is fixed, so the body color has to agree with it.

See exterior services
  • Body: warm white, light gray or mid-tone greige, satin
  • Trim: crisp white or a shade darker than the body
  • Front door: deep green, black, navy or brick red
  • Shutters: match the door or go a shade darker
  • Porch ceiling: haint blue, because it is the River Valley
