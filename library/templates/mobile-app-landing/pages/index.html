Why it sticks

Built for the first nine weeks, not the marathon.

Six things Pace does differently, because the hard part of running is the first month, not the first mile.

Walk-run intervals

Week one is sixty seconds of jogging, ninety of walking, eight times. Nobody runs a mile on day one. Nobody needs to.

A coach, not a cheerleader

Plain spoken cues over your own music or podcast. "Walk now. Breathe. Forty seconds." No whooping, no confetti.

Missed a week? Fine.

Life happens. Skip four days and the plan quietly repeats the last week instead of pushing you into the next one.

Phone stays home

Apple Watch and Garmin apps run the whole workout from your wrist and sync when you are back.

Progress you can feel

No pace charts on day one. Instead: how long you ran without stopping, and how that compares to three weeks ago.

Week nine: 5K

The plan ends with thirty minutes of continuous running. Then a 10K plan, a half plan, or just keep running thirty minutes forever.
