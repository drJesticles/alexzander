Safety, in plain English

The four things we get asked about most.

What Current Electric Co. tells every homeowner about the wiring in older River Valley houses. Expand any section.

Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels

Thousands of homes built between 1950 and 1990 around Russellville still have Federal Pacific "Stab-Lok" or Zinsco panels. Their breakers are documented to fail to trip under a fault, which is how a short becomes a fire. Many insurers will no longer write a policy on a house that has one.

If the label on your panel says either name, call us. A replacement is a one-day job from $3,200, and we handle the permit, inspection and the letter your insurance company wants.

Aluminum wiring

Houses wired between about 1965 and 1973 often have aluminum branch wiring. It expands and loosens at connections over time, which shows up as warm cover plates, flickering and the smell of hot plastic. You do not always need a full rewire; approved COPALUM or AlumiConn connectors at every device bring it up to safe, and we price that per outlet.

GFCI and AFCI protection

GFCI outlets cut power in a fraction of a second when current leaks to ground, which is what saves you in a kitchen, bathroom, garage or outdoors. AFCI breakers catch the arcing behind walls that starts fires. Current code wants both in most of the house.

Adding them to an older home does not require a rewire. GFCI outlets are $95 each installed; AFCI breakers are $120 each, and we test every one before we leave.

Smoke and CO detectors

Detectors expire. Smoke alarms are good for ten years, CO alarms for five to seven, and the date is printed on the back. Arkansas code wants a smoke alarm in every bedroom, outside every sleeping area and on every level, all interconnected so they sound together. We install hardwired, interconnected units with battery backup from $85 each.
