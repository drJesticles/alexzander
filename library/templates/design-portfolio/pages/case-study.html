Fjord Audio: an identity that works at 4 mm and 4 metres

By Elin Marren, Elin Marren studio · June 2026 · Identity · Packaging · Web

Fjord makes small, expensive speakers. Their old mark was a photograph of a wave that fell apart on a 4 mm badge and looked like a bank at trade-show size. The brief was one word: coherence.

The problem

Fjord had grown from one product to eleven in four years. Each launch had its own packaging, its own type and its own version of the logo. Retail buyers could not tell the entry speaker from the flagship, and neither could the website.

The constraint that decided everything

The smallest place the mark appears is a 4 mm badge on a Bluetooth earpiece. The largest is a 4 metre banner at the Munich High End show. One drawing had to survive both. That ruled out the wave, the gradient and anything with a counter smaller than a stroke.

We stopped asking what the logo should look like and started asking what it could survive.

From the project kickoff notes

What we made

A single geometric F built on a 6-unit grid, a two-weight grotesque for everything else, and a packaging system where the product tier is carried by paper stock and one printed numeral, not by a new layout each time. The website reuses the same grid; the product pages are literally the packaging unfolded.

Results after six months

  • Retail packaging cost down 18 percent through two shared box sizes instead of eleven.
  • Product page conversion up 31 percent against the previous site, same traffic.
  • Zero logo variants in circulation. One file, four colours, every surface.

The system was handed over as a 40-page guide and a Figma library. Fjord's two-person in-house team has launched three products since without a call to the studio, which is the real measure of whether an identity works.
