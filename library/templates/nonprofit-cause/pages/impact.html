How we got here

From a church closet to a refrigerated truck.

  1. 2011

    A closet and a sign-up sheet

    Four women from three churches start a Saturday pantry in a supply closet on Arkansas Avenue. First month: 23 families, mostly canned goods from their own kitchens.

  2. 2014

    The backpack program

    A Russellville school counselor mentions kids hoarding cafeteria food on Fridays. We start with 40 backpacks at one school. It is 600 at 14 schools now.

  3. 2018

    The building

    A donor buys the old feed store next door and gives it to us. The pantry goes from a closet to 4,000 square feet with a walk-in cooler and a loading dock.

  4. 2021

    The truck

    A used refrigerated box truck, bought with a grant and 340 small gifts, starts the mobile route. Dover and Hector are the first stops. Pounds distributed double in a year.

  5. 2025

    1.2 million pounds

    Seven towns, 4,100 households, 620 volunteers, and a second cooler. Still four part-time staff. Still a sign-up sheet on the wall by the door.

In their words

Guests, volunteers and partners.

$25 feeds a family of four for a week.

Every dollar given to River Valley Food Project buys about nine meals through our wholesale and gleaning partners. Monthly gifts keep the trucks running in July, when donations drop and need does not.
