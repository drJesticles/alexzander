How it works

Two walls, one fire, almost no smoke.

Smoke is unburned fuel. Hearth One feeds hot air back into the top of the fire so it burns a second time before it ever leaves the pit.

The airflow

Cold air enters through a ring of holes at the base, splits, and travels two ways: straight up through the fire grate, and up the gap between the inner and outer walls. The wall air heats to several hundred degrees on the way and exits through 24 jets just under the rim, where it meets the smoke rising off the fire and ignites it.

The result is the ring of small flames you see at the top of the pit a few minutes after lighting. That ring is the smoke burning. What is left is mostly heat and carbon dioxide, which is why you can sit downwind.

The materials

Both walls are 2 mm 304 stainless, the same grade used in commercial kitchens. It will darken to a bronze colour after the first few fires and stay there. There is no paint or coating to flake, and the pit can be left outside under the lid year round.

The ash pan, grate and stand are the same steel. Every weld is done by hand in Fort Smith and every pit is burned once before it ships so the first fire you see is not the first fire it has seen.
