Storm damage

Hail or wind just came through. Here is what to do in the next 48 hours.

Summit Ridge Roofing has worked every major hail and wind event in Russellville since 2012. The homeowners who come out ahead are the ones who document first and sign nothing in the driveway.

Do this first

Photograph everything from the ground: dented gutters, torn screens, shingles in the yard, dings on the AC unit and the mailbox. Note the date and time of the storm. If water is coming in, put a bucket under it and call us; we tarp active leaks the same day at no charge if we do the repair.

Then call your insurance company and open a claim. You do not need an estimate to open one. Ask for the claim number and the adjuster's name, and tell them you have a local roofer who will meet them on site.

Do not do this

Do not sign anything with a crew that knocks on your door, especially an "assignment of benefits" or a contingency agreement. It hands your claim to a company that may be gone by the time the roof leaks. Do not let anyone on your roof without proof of insurance in hand.

Do not accept the first adjuster number as final. Roofs get partially approved all the time because the adjuster missed a slope. A written inspection from a local roofer, with photos, is how a partial becomes a full replacement.

How a claim goes

Three steps from storm to new roof, with us doing the paperwork.

  1. Free storm inspection

    We walk every slope, chalk the hail hits, photograph the collateral damage and write it up in the format adjusters expect.

  3. We meet the adjuster

    Your foreman is on the roof when the adjuster is, pointing out what we found. Approvals go up when someone local is there to show the damage.

  5. New roof, deductible only

    We build to the approved scope, handle the supplement if something was missed, and you pay your deductible. Nothing more, by law.
