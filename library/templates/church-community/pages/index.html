A church for people who are not sure about church.

Riverside Community Church is 400 or so people on Knoxville Avenue who sing loud, ask honest questions, and show up for each other during the week. Come as you are. We mean that literally.

What to expect

Sunday, start to finish.

Both services at Riverside Community Church are the same: about 75 minutes, four or five songs, a 30-minute sermon from the Bible, and coffee before and after.

9:00 and 11:00

Same sermon, same songs. The 9:00 is quieter; the 11:00 is fuller and has more families with small kids.

Kids and students

Nursery through 5th grade meet during both services, with background-checked volunteers. Students meet Wednesday nights.

Coffee is free and good

Bluebird roasts it for us. The lobby opens 30 minutes before each service and nobody will make you stand up and introduce yourself.

Preaching from the Bible

We work through a book at a time, a chapter or so each week. Right now it is the Gospel of Mark. You can pick up anywhere.

Music you can sing

A band with guitars and a piano, hymns and newer songs, words on the screen. Loud enough that you will not hear yourself if you are shy.

Wear whatever

Jeans, boots, a suit, scrubs from a night shift. The pastor usually wears a flannel. Nobody is looking at your clothes.

Who we are

Started in a living room in 2003. Still trying to act like one.

Riverside Community Church began when eleven people met in the Hargroves' living room on Skyline Drive to read the Bible and eat together. We bought the old gym on Knoxville Avenue in 2009 and have been slowly fixing it up since. About 400 people call this home now, from Russellville, Dardanelle, Atkins, Dover and London.

  • Rooted in the Bible. We preach through books of Scripture and believe what the historic creeds say about Jesus.
  • Built on small groups. Thirty groups meet in homes during the week. That is where most of the real church happens.
  • Here for the town. We give away 20% of what comes in: the food pantry, the school backpack drive, the River Valley shelter.
